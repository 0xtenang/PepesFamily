How the model works

  • Prices. Thirty coins are listed at a time. Every 10 minutes each coin moves randomly around its own trend. Pumps multiply the price by 1.5 to 3 in one step; rugs drop it to 5–15%.
  • Listings. A coin that rugs, or falls 80% below its listing price, stops taking new bets and is replaced by a fresh coin. Liquidity starts around 120 SOL (20 to 3,000) and moves with the square root of price, as in a DEX pool.
  • Traders. Bets arrive at random. Stakes vary around the average, and hold times are random around the average hold. Trend readers bet on the side each coin is really heading. Insiders know a pump or rug is coming and bet the coin's full room at the highest leverage it allows.
  • Same markets for both columns. Each run feeds identical prices and traders to both rule sets, so differences come from the rules alone.
  • Where Inverse's page is silent, I assumed: the pool's fee share also covers the profit fee and the 15% liquidation fee; max win stays +400% below 100 SOL of pool; the per-coin limit counts position size (stake × leverage); the hourly loss pause and the 30-second fair-fill average are not modeled; a hedge costs 0.5% each time it is resized.
  • LPs never deposit or withdraw during a run, so the pool's value change is their return.
